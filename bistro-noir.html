The House

A room built for
long evenings

Bistro Noir began in 2014 in a converted printworks off Aberdeen Street. We kept the walls dark, the lighting low and the room small on purpose — twelve tables is enough to know every guest by name, and enough for the kitchen to cook without compromise.

The menu changes with the market and the tide. Nothing arrives frozen; the turbot is line-caught, the vegetables are picked that morning in Yuen Long, and the sauces are built over three days. What reaches the table is quiet, precise, and unmistakably French.

Kenji Nakamura Executive Chef & Partner

The Chef

Kenji Nakamura

“A great plate should feel inevitable. You should not be able to imagine it any other way — and you should never see the work behind it.”
Kenji Nakamura

Kenji trained for six years in Kyoto before moving to Lyon, where he spent a further five years in the kitchens of two Michelin-starred houses. He arrived in Hong Kong in 2011 and opened Bistro Noir three years later with a single conviction: that French technique and Japanese restraint belong on the same plate.

He still cooks service every night, and still writes the menu by hand each morning after the market — usually before the first delivery arrives.
